The Current Challenge

What most organizations are navigating today.

Organizations are expected to improve quality of life, participant engagement, and workforce readiness while operating with fewer staff and tighter budgets. Most want to provide meaningful, consistent experiences for the people they serve. Few have the time, staffing, or systems to deliver them. That gap shows up every day.

1
Access is inconsistent and dependent on family resources
When organizations leave personal care to families, access depends on who has the time and money. Many people get nothing.
2
Coordination consumes staff time that should go elsewhere
Every hour a staff member spends coordinating outside providers is an hour not spent on the people they were hired to serve.
3
Specialized care requires specialized preparation
Most outside providers are not trained to work with people who have cognitive, physical, behavioral, or sensory needs. Cousins professionals collect participant information before every visit and adapt accordingly.
4
Staff are performing work outside their primary role
Activities teams become schedulers, coordinators, transportation managers, and personal appearance providers. Every responsibility added to an already full role reduces capacity for the work the organization actually hired them to do.
5
Programs disappear when one person leaves
When a program depends on a single employee, volunteer, or outside contact, it stops the moment that person is unavailable. There is no documentation, no handoff, and no continuity. The program has to be rebuilt from scratch.
▸
What most organizations try instead: outsourcing to local stylists with no accountability structure, asking staff to manage logistics, leaving access entirely to family resources, or running one-time events. Each approach addresses one problem while creating others. The common thread is that none of them produce a consistent, documented, recurring program your team can rely on.
6
Large employers overlook personal care as a workforce investment
How employees look and feel affects how they work. Organizations with high turnover and low engagement rarely invest in the personal experience of their workforce. A company that does is telling its people they matter. That changes things.
Why not build this internally?

Many organizations try to coordinate these programs with existing staff. Over time, scheduling becomes inconsistent, responsibilities shift, and programs stall when anyone is unavailable. Cousins provides the people, systems, and processes needed to keep programs running without expanding internal workload. We help organizations deliver more consistently without adding staff, systems, or operational burden.
